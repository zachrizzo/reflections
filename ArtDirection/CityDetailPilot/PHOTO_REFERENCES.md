# Gray London photo references

Use December 2008 as the visual baseline for the Great Tower Street / Mark Lane junction and September 2008 for the Leadenhall Market finish. Use the 1974 archive image to reconstruct the permanent masonry of 60–61 Mark Lane. Place each identifiable building against its own footprint.

## Great Tower Street / Mark Lane

- **Looking up Mark Lane.** The left side starts with tall beige-gray stone office arches and deep, dark set-back openings, above which the windows form vertical strips. On the right, a dark green-gray glazed office corner gives way to older stone façades with projecting bays. A taller glazed building closes the view. The lane reads as a narrow one-way asphalt street with raised slab footways, a broad apron around the near left corner, a curbside grate and double yellow lines. The red pillar box sits in the apron as a small foreground accent. [Mike Quinn, north view, 17 Dec 2008, CC BY-SA 2.0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mark_Lane,_EC3_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1093027.jpg)
- **Looking back across the junction.** Use the red cast-iron pillar box with black plinth, wide rounded cap and two slots. The reverse view includes a glazed office with a rounded corner, the dark masonry and green spire of All Hallows by the Tower, an ordinary streetlight, traffic signals and crossing rails. The red and green accents stand out because most of the street is neutral. [Mike Quinn, south view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Great_Tower_Street_-_Mark_Lane,_EC3_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1093032.jpg) · [Mike Quinn, pillar box close-up](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Victorian_postbox,_Great_Tower_Street_-_Mark_Lane,_EC3_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1093018.jpg)
- **60–61 Mark Lane.** George Aitchison’s four-storey 1864 building on the east side is the first custom historic façade worth modeling. The archive view shows a short run of narrow arched bays contained between contrasting neighboring plots. Use pale Portland stone, a main entrance recessed between paired columns, shallow segmental arches at street level, deeper arcades above, tight carved capitals, horizontal relief bands and a flat cornice. The photo is monochrome, so use the listing for the stone type and use the image for form and contrast. [The London Archives, LCC Photograph Library, 1974, SC_PHL_01_017_74_10995, research use](https://www.londonpicturearchive.org.uk/embed-item?i=51737) · [Historic England, Grade II* listing 1359196](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1359196)
- **Great Tower Street variation.** Develop separate forms for the classical stone and granite of No. 1, with its curved projecting bays, and the red brick, Portland stone dressings, tiled mansard and dormers of No. 27. Use these records for their individual addresses. The City’s Eastcheap description also gives context for the local mix of warehouses, offices and narrow historic plots and the eastward sightline toward All Hallows and the Tower. [City of London, Eastcheap SPD, pp. 14–16](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/eastcheap-conservation-area-character-summary-management-strategy.pdf) · [Historic England, No. 27](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1064672)

**Change the concept.** Replace the continuous decorative retail row with the photographed mix of masonry offices, sparse retail and modern glazing. Reuse the pilot’s painted signs where a mapped unit is plausible for gameplay. Give corner arches real depth, vary upper-floor recession and roof forms by building, then add the junction postbox, lane marks and directional sightlines. Keep asphalt on the carriageway and reserve slabs for the footways.

## Leadenhall Market via Lime Street Passage

- **Approach.** Follow the mapped turns and compressed entry into the south market gate. The City records simple brick extensions along Lime Street Passage and individually sized carved Portland stone entrance pediments. The historic market mass rises two to three storeys internally against the taller office backdrop. Block out the approach using the City’s views 3 and 15 before placing the gate or changing the footprint. [City of London, Leadenhall Market SPD, pp. 14–17 and 20](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/leadenhall-market-spd-conservation-area.pdf)
- **Inside the gate.** The 2008 photo shows a wide band of dark granite setts between smoother, narrower paved shop margins. The structure reads in repeated tall painted supports and ribs, with daylight falling through the high glazed roof. Use deep red cornice and column bases, warm cream upper panels, dark green-blue timber glazing and brass or ochre accents. Model the large arched gateway, clerestory glazing, hanging lanterns and cast-iron details. Keep rich carving concentrated at entrances and the central crossing. [Peter McDermott, 14 Sep 2008, CC BY-SA 2.0](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/969986) · [Historic England, Grade II* market listing 1286182](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1286182) · [City of London, Leadenhall Market SPD, materials and public realm, p. 30](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/leadenhall-market-spd-conservation-area.pdf)

**Build in two passes.** First model the actual portal, shop bay rhythm, glass roof and transition from the narrow approach into the bright arcade. Then add selected carving, hanging lamps, signage and shop displays. Image-to-3D can help prototype unique carved capitals, heraldic panels or a complete bespoke façade using suitably licensed references. Check generated silhouettes and openings against the source views and build the walkable envelope to the measured map footprint.

## Dimensions and verification

The photos support relative widths and depths, including a narrower junction carriageway between raised footways, deep office arch reveals, and the passage opening up into a broader arcade. The photo proportions are perspective estimates. The existing four-metre shopfront module is a project dimension. Measure the building frontage, door and aisle clearances against mapped footprints and Unreal traces before setting final geometry. Save matched screenshots at the Mark Lane mouth looking north and south, and at the Lime Street Passage threshold looking into the market.
